## What is the Lift Angle in a Watch?

Watch lift angles are an element of timepieces that to experts and seasoned watchmakers are easy to understand but to newbies and novices **[entering the world of horology and watchmaking](https://watchmakingtools.com/watchmaking-resources/how-to-become-a-watchmaker/)**, it can be a complex mechanic to wrap your head around.

The balance wheel in a watch swings back and forth and in the middle of each swing, the impulse pin at the center enters and exits the pallet fork. A lift angle on a watch refers to the angle between the connection and release of the impulse pin.

For the easiest way to understand how it works, the best example is to picture a swing. When you push it, it swings back and forth. Similarly, the balance wheel in a watch swings back and forth in a controlled manner. Now imagine the swing was low and your feet touched the ground in the middle of each swing. The lift angle is the distance between when your feet make contact with the ground and leave contact with the ground again.

The lift angle is essentially the angle between the two connection points and in horology, it is crucial to know the lift angle of your timepiece to precisely determine its accuracy. It is particularly important to know your lift angle when [**using a timegrapher**](https://watchmakingtools.com/beginners-guide-to-timegraphers/) to get the most accurate readings. Understanding lift angles helps us appreciate how these tiny movements play a big role in keeping our watches on time.

## How to Determine the Lift Angle of Your Watch Movement

You will find that most watches have a lift angle between 48° and 54° with most [timegraphers](https://watchmakingtools.com/watchmakers-tools/timegraphers/ "Timegraphers") preset at a default of 52°. If you are unsure of the lift angle of your watch, of course, you can go ahead and use the default of 52° as your guesstimate but this will not achieve the most precise and accurate readings. For most watches, it does not matter greatly if your lift angle is off by a degree or two but your readings will still be slightly unreliable.

For some **[vintage watch movements](https://watchmakingtools.com/movements/)**, however, the readings could end up way off. For example, Valjoux calibers have a lift angle of just 42° and if you were to use the default, you would find that the amplitude of the timepiece would be extremely inaccurate.

If you do want your readings to be true and accurate, you need to discover your lift angle and there are several ways in which you can find this. To determine the lift angle of your watch movement, there are two different ways. Firstly, the most simple one is to look it up. However, if you are up for more of a challenge or are working with an unknown watch movement, you may need to discover the lift angle of your timepiece with the help of a timegrapher.

## How to Find the Lift Angle of Your Watch

Firstly, you can look it up. With a world of technology at our fingertips, it is incredibly easy to find out any information that we require and this includes the lift angle of your watch. There are many lists scattered across the internet that keep up-to-date and accurate lists of lift angles for each watch movement including right here at WatchmakingTools.

Below you will find an extensive list of watch lift angles for a vast array of watch movements. They are sorted in alphabetical order of watch movements so simply find your watch movement within the list and you will discover the lift angle alongside the beat rate/frequency (BPH).

Here at WatchmakingTools we have one of the most comprehensive lists of lift angles you will find on the internet, compiled via movement technical manuals and other trusted sources. The list will continue to be updated and expanded over time but if you find that your watch movement is not present, let us know. In the meantime, there is an alternative way that you can determine your lift angle.

## How to Determine the Lift Angle With a Timegrapher

An alternative option to looking it up is also available if you fancy the challenge of figuring it out or have an extremely rare watch that you simply cannot find the information about. It will also be your only option if you are working with an unknown watch movement. For this, you will already need to know the amplitude of your watch.

Make sure to mark one of the balance arms to highlight the position at rest and then slowly turn the mainspring until it reaches an amplitude of 180°. You can check this with the help of the slow-motion setting on a smartphone camera as you can physically check with a slowed down recording if it is 180°.

Once you are at this point, pop your timepiece onto a timegrapher and adjust the lift angle setting until the amplitude shows at 180°. When it reads as 180°, you have found your lift angle.

The lift angle is the distance between the entry and exit point of an impulse pin when the balance wheel swings.

**How to determine watch lift angle**

The best starting point is to check the technical documentation or specifications provided by the watch movement’s manufacturer. The second easiest option is to use a timegrapher.

**What is the lift angle in a watch?**

The lift angle is the angle through which a watch’s balance wheel rotates before it unlocks the escapement, allowing the gear train to advance. The lift angle affects a watch’s accuracy, power reserve, and overall performance.
