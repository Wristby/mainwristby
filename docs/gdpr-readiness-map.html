WRISTBY · Data protection

GDPR readiness map

What the app stores, where information flows, what is already protected, and what to verify before automating retention or deletion.

Prepared 22 October 2026 · Based on source-code review, official public guidance, and the owner's answers

This is a readiness assessment, not a legal opinion or a compliance certificate. It describes the application and reported business practices. It does not inspect customer records, credentials, the live hosting account, or provider contracts. The business remains responsible for confirming its legal obligations and operating procedures with an appropriate Dutch accountant or privacy adviser.

Executive summary

EU customers Mostly consumers Production reported on cPanel No customer records inspected

WRISTBY is a private inventory and customer-relationship app. It associates client profiles with buying and selling watches, sales totals, service history, deliveries, and expense records. The owner says customers are based in the EU and are mostly end users, the seller offers a 12-month warranty, the live app and database are in a cPanel environment, and shipping details are entered into UPS separately.

The app has useful baseline protections: private sign-in, public registration disabled, authenticated API routes, non-cacheable API responses, and request/error logs designed to omit customer data. These controls do not establish GDPR compliance by themselves.

The main open work is to establish a defensible purpose and retention period for each data category; accurately inform customers; handle their rights; prevent deletion of accounting or warranty evidence where it must be preserved; and verify hosting, backups, and provider contracts.

Important warranty clarification

Do not rely on “12 months is the Dutch legal minimum” without checking the terms. ACM ConsuWijzer says Dutch statutory conformity rights have no single fixed time limit; they depend on how long a buyer could reasonably expect the product to last. A 12-month seller warranty is a separate promise and does not necessarily end a consumer's statutory rights.

EU guidance describes a general two-year legal guarantee and allows a shorter agreed period for second-hand goods only in some countries, not below one year where that option exists. Because WRISTBY sells mainly to EU consumers, check the buyer's country, whether the watch is second-hand, and the exact warranty wording. This map does not decide whether a particular 12-month policy is lawful.

Practical effect on data retention: confirm what records are needed to administer the seller's warranty and respond to statutory consumer claims, and when those obligations end. Store the seller's own warranty promise separately from the legal/conformity rights that may apply.

External data flows and hosting

  • cPanel: the owner says the live app and database are in a cPanel environment. cPanel is hosting-control-panel software; the company that operates and bills for the actual server is the provider whose service terms, security measures, data location, and backup policy must be checked. The workspace's .replit file still contains a Replit Autoscale deployment setting, so that file is not evidence of the live cPanel server or its region.
  • UPS: the owner says customer details are entered directly when arranging shipping. This is an external handoff, not an API transfer from WRISTBY. Send only the fields needed for the shipment, include the relevant recipient/service in the customer information, and check UPS's current EEA privacy notice and applicable terms. Do not assume UPS is merely a processor; its notice describes controller responsibilities for its own purposes.
  • Google Gemini: the app makes authenticated calls to Google's Gemini API for watch listing text, captions, and movement-spec lookup. The built-in requests contain watch attributes such as brand, model/reference, year, condition, accessories, and sometimes list price—not buyer name, email, or phone. Admin-editable prompts are another input path, so never place client contact details or other personal data in those templates. Verify the API service/tier, current data-use terms, processing addendum, region, and retention before including any personal data.
  • Google Drive: WRISTBY can store and open a Drive folder link for a watch; it does not upload the folder contents through an in-app Drive API. If a linked folder contains invoices, warranty files, or customer contact information, Drive becomes part of the records and must be included in the privacy/provider review.
  • Photos: the database schema has a text-array image field, but no in-app upload or photo-storage flow was found in the reviewed source. Check whether this legacy field is populated and whether existing photo URLs or linked files reveal identifiable information.
Hosting, backups, and provider contracts are unverified. No production console, database rows, secret values, account credentials, or backups were accessed. The underlying host, server/data region, backup locations and expiry, TLS/at-rest encryption, recovery tests, and written agreements need provider documentation or confirmation.

Controls already visible in code

  • Public registration is denied; data APIs require an authenticated session and an existing app user.
  • The password-based login uses a server-side PostgreSQL session store and bcrypt password verification. Session cookies are configured HttpOnly, SameSite Lax, secure in production, and expire after one week.
  • Write requests are restricted to same-origin; API responses use Cache-Control: no-store. Security headers include content-type protection and a restrictive referrer policy.
  • Request logs are limited to route/method/status/timing and omit request bodies, query strings, headers, cookies, and user-provided IDs. The shared error handler avoids logging raw error objects.

Still to verify: HTTPS/TLS enforcement and HSTS at the actual cPanel proxy; database and backup encryption; host staff/support access; backups and restoration; monitoring/log retention at the hosting provider; multi-user and MFA needs; and access/audit history for changes to client records. The code-level session settings alone do not prove these hosting controls are active.

Medium There is no in-app privacy-request log, complete client export, approved retention schedule, legal hold, or auditable erasure process. Design an owner-only process with minimal identity checks, response reminders, decisions/retention exceptions, and a record of completion. The general response period is one month; applicable extensions need timely notice.
Medium Accounting retention, warranty evidence, optional profile data, tracking details, and backup retention are not separated into approved schedules. Map retention to record type, purpose, and trigger date. Do not automatically apply an accounting period to every contact field or set deletion dates before advice.

The Dutch Tax Administration describes seven years for many core records and ten years for some specified categories. That is a rule for applicable administrative records, not a blanket instruction to retain every personal profile field for seven years. Have an accountant identify which WRISTBY records are actually part of the required administration and when each period starts.

Prioritised feature roadmap

Phase 0 Confirm the policy and providers

Prepare a compact record-of-processing inventory: purpose, data subjects, categories, source, access, recipients, lawful-basis candidate, retention trigger/period, security, and transfer. Confirm controller identity/contact, exact EU customer countries, consumer versus business sales, warranty terms, accounting categories, actual host/database/backup providers, and Gemini/Drive/UPS arrangements. Screen whether a formal DPIA is necessary. Have legal/accounting decisions reviewed before encoding them.

Phase 1 Minimise data and inform customers

Keep only fields with a confirmed purpose; make optional profile fields clearly optional and guide users not to enter sensitive or unnecessary details in free-text notes. Provide a clear privacy notice at or before collection with controller contact, purposes, basis, recipients, periods, rights, and complaint route. A consent checkbox is not a substitute for choosing the correct lawful basis. Add a cookie banner only if the live app uses non-essential cookies or similar tracking.

Phase 2 Handle privacy requests on time

Add an owner-only request log for access, rectification, restriction, objection, portability, and erasure; track received date, verification completed without saving copies of identity papers, response due date, extension/reason where permitted, outcome, and closure. Generate a secure export covering Client fields and linked buyer/seller transactions, while screening other people's information and statutory exceptions. The normal response period is one month.

Phase 3 Apply approved retention and legal holds

Separate optional contact/profile data from customer identity and evidence actually required for accounting, warranty, or legal claims. After periods and start dates are approved, apply per-category retention, legal holds, scheduled review, documented deletion or anonymisation, and backup expiry. Preserve required records without keeping every optional detail indefinitely. Do not automate destructive cleanup until policies and recovery are signed off.

Phase 4 Protect access, suppliers, and response

Confirm HTTPS, encryption, least-privilege access, MFA, session revocation, and backup restoration at the real host. Keep a small audit trail of access/change actions without copying client data into logs. Verify written processor terms and transfers for the actual host, Google services, Drive, and UPS; keep a breach-response register/checklist with escalation and the conditional 72-hour supervisory-authority notification rule.

Owner and adviser decisions before implementation

  1. The legal name/contact details of the business responsible for the client records, and the channel clients should use for privacy requests.
  2. The exact countries in which consumer sales are made, and whether sales are actively offered across borders.
  3. The seller's 12-month warranty wording, how it interacts with statutory consumer rights, how second-hand condition is disclosed, and what evidence is needed when a claim is raised.
  4. Which financial records form part of Dutch or other applicable accounting records; their approved retention trigger and period; and any litigation or warranty holds.
  5. The actual hosting company, server and database regions, backup provider/locations/expiry, encryption and restoration terms. cPanel's own documentation explains that it supplies hosting software; identify the company that actually hosts the website.
  6. Whether Google Drive folders hold invoices or customer records; the Google Gemini service/tier and current terms; and the exact personal fields entered into UPS and its terms. Provider roles and any international transfers must be checked from the applicable contracts and notices.
